While today’s condition of architecture is faced with the question of reuse, addressing existing structures opens a field for experimentation.

This condition risks reducing architecture to constrained decisions, where form becomes either abstract or diminished.

Historical precedents such as Bramante’s Tempietto or Terragni’s “Biological Pillar” offer pragmatic responses.

However, by integrating reuse as one element among others, they often dissolve the tension of the project.

Rather than seeking freedom within limitation, incompleteness becomes a positive argument for form to question its boundaries.

The “Colonne-cadre” explores this condition, rethinking the relation between project and context.

Developed in research on the Lemanic arc, it reveals spatial potential through transformation.

It becomes a principle for a composite construction, proposing new typological and spatial relationships.
